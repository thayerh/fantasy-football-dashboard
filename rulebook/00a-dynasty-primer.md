# 00a — Dynasty Primer

**This subsection is not rules.** Nothing here is binding and nothing here overrides the constitution. It exists so that an owner who has never played dynasty can sit down at the startup draft knowing what they are doing, and so that anyone can look up why this league is shaped the way it is. Where this page and a numbered document disagree, the numbered document wins.

## What dynasty is

In a redraft league, everyone throws their team back in the pot every August and starts over. In a dynasty league, **you keep your entire roster forever.** There is no annual redraft, no keeper limit, no reset. The team you build in the startup draft is the team you own until you trade or cut your way into a different one.

Three things follow from that, and they change almost everything:

**Every player has a value beyond this season.** A 23-year-old wide receiver coming off a quiet rookie year is worth more than a 31-year-old putting up better numbers right now, because you are buying the next six years, not the next twelve weeks. Redraft rankings will steer you wrong constantly for exactly this reason.

**There is an annual rookie draft.** Every spring, after the NFL draft, this league holds a [4-round rookie draft](06-rookie-draft.md) for that year's incoming class. Those picks are tradeable up to three drafts out, which means "a 2029 first" is a real asset you can buy and sell today.

**Trading is the engine, not a side activity.** In redraft, most teams make two or three trades a year. In dynasty, trading is how rosters actually change — the waiver wire is thin because 276 players are already rostered, so the good players are all on someone's team. This league requires [one trade per season to be payout-eligible](08-trades.md#88-the-annual-trade-requirement) for that reason.

## The one idea that matters most: contending or rebuilding

Most dynasty mistakes come from teams that will not admit which one they are.

**Contending** means you think you can win this year. You trade future picks and young players for proven production now. You are shortening your window on purpose, and that is fine — championships are the point.

**Rebuilding** means you have accepted that this year is gone. You trade your aging productive players to contenders for picks and young talent while those players still have value. The mistake is not rebuilding; the mistake is waiting until your 30-year-old running back has nothing left before you sell him.

**The worst place to be is the middle** — a roster too old to grow and too weak to win, whose owner keeps making small moves to stay 6-8. If you are honestly not going to make a run, commit to the rebuild early. If you are close, be aggressive. In a league that never resets, indecision is the only strategy that reliably fails.

Note that rebuilding is explicitly legitimate here and **tanking is not** — see [§9.3](09-governance-and-conduct.md#93-tanking-and-lineup-integrity). Trade your veterans for picks all you like; you still have to start your best available lineup every week.

## What is different about *this* league

Generic dynasty advice will get you close. These four things are specific and will cost you if you ignore them.

### Superflex makes quarterbacks the most valuable asset in the league

You start [ten players](02-rosters-and-lineups.md#21-starting-lineup), and one slot — SUPERFLEX — can be a second quarterback. You are never required to start two, but you will want to, because a starting NFL quarterback outscores almost any flex option.

Twelve teams trying to start two QBs means **24 starting quarterbacks in a league where maybe 20 are genuinely startable.** Quarterbacks go far earlier than any redraft ranking suggests, young ones cost a fortune in trade, and a team that punts the position spends years digging out. If you take one lesson from this page: in the startup, do not be the team that waits.

### Three mandatory WR slots make receivers the second scarcest thing

Three WR slots plus two flexes across twelve teams means your third receiver is, leaguewide, roughly the 36th-best at the position — a player who would be waiver fodder in a shallower league. Draft depth at receiver accordingly, and note that WRs age better than running backs, which makes them the safest long-term asset class in dynasty.

### Running backs are the trap

Running backs peak early, fall off a cliff around 27, and get hurt. They also score a lot right now, which makes them feel indispensable. The standard dynasty error is paying a young-player price for a 28-year-old back. Sell them a year early rather than a year late — you will feel bad about it and you will be right.

### Your $100 FAAB has to last a full calendar year

The budget is issued once, [does not roll over, and does not reset until the following rookie draft](07-waivers-and-free-agency.md#71-faab-budget). Waivers run year-round, including the offseason. An owner who empties the budget filling their roster in August cannot claim anyone in March. In year one especially, remember you leave the startup three players short and have to fill those spots out of the same $100.

## The startup draft

This is the single most consequential event in the league's history and it runs [20 rounds in one 2.5-hour sitting](05-startup-draft.md#53-timing). A few practical things:

- **Queue at least 45 players.** Rounds 11–20 are on a 15-second clock. That is enough time to confirm a queued pick and nothing else. Owners who do not queue end up on autopick, and autopick uses redraft-flavored rankings that are wrong for a dynasty superflex league.
- **Rookies are in the pool.** There is no separate rookie draft in year one — this year's incoming class is on the same board as everyone else.
- **Both strategies work.** "Draft young and win in three years" and "draft the best players and win now" are both legitimate openings. What does not work is doing half of each by accident.
- **No trading until the final pick.** Not before, not during. Nobody can price anything yet, which is exactly why.

## Rookie picks are currency

A first-round rookie pick in a superflex league is a genuinely valuable asset, and picks two and three years out trade constantly. Some orientation:

- Picks 1.01–1.06 go to the six teams that missed the playoffs, in [reverse order of the regular-season standings](06-rookie-draft.md#62-draft-order). Picks 1.07–1.12 go to playoff teams, decided by placement games.
- **Future picks are cheap to sell and expensive to buy back.** A rebuilding team should be accumulating them; a contender should be spending them. Just know that "my 2029 first" feels free right now and will not in 2029.
- You cannot trade a future pick if you have not paid your dues ([§8.3](08-trades.md#83-trading-draft-picks)).

## The taxi squad, briefly

Three slots, [rookies only](02-rosters-and-lineups.md#23-taxi-squad). Players parked there do not count against your 23-man active roster and cannot be started. It exists so that stashing a developmental rookie does not cost you a bench spot you need in September.

The rule that surprises everyone: **promotion is one-way.** You can pull a taxi player up into your lineup at any point in the season, but you can never send him back down. If your third-round rookie breaks out in Week 6, use him — you just cannot undo it.

## How to actually trade

The most common reason a dynasty league dies is that nobody trades. Some things that help:

- **Send real offers, not fishing expeditions.** "Anyone interested in my RB?" gets ignored. A specific two-for-one with a name on it gets a response.
- **Answer every offer, even to decline.** A one-word "no thanks" within a day is the whole social contract here, and it is in [§9.6](09-governance-and-conduct.md#96-conduct).
- **Value differences are the point.** Trades happen because a rebuilder and a contender price a 29-year-old differently. If everyone agreed on values, nothing would ever trade.
- **There are no vetoes.** Trades process automatically and the commissioner reverses only for [collusion](08-trades.md#86-collusion), which means a trade you think is bad. A lopsided-looking trade is not grounds for anything.

## Common first-year mistakes

1. **Punting quarterback in a superflex startup.** The most expensive error available to you.
2. **Using redraft rankings.** They ignore age entirely. Use dynasty-specific values, set to superflex.
3. **Overvaluing your own rookies.** Everyone thinks their third-rounder is the next star. Most are not.
4. **Spending the whole FAAB budget in August.** It has to cover twelve months.
5. **Refusing to sell a declining veteran** because you drafted him and he was good once.
6. **Going quiet.** The rulebook requires one trade a season for a payout, but the real cost of disengaging is that your roster ages in place while everyone else's improves.

## Helpful websites

Value and trade tools — **set every one of these to Superflex before you trust a number.** They default to 1QB, and 1QB values are badly wrong for this league.

| Site | What it's good for |
|---|---|
| [KeepTradeCut](https://keeptradecut.com) | Crowdsourced dynasty values and a trade calculator. The most widely used common language for "is this fair?" |
| [FantasyCalc](https://fantasycalc.com) | Values derived from trades actually executed in real leagues, configurable for superflex and roster size |
| [DynastyProcess](https://dynastyprocess.com) | Free value charts and calculators, data-driven and updated often |
| [Dynasty League Football](https://dynastyleaguefootball.com) | Long-running dynasty site: rankings, rookie profiles, startup ADP |
| [Dynasty Nerds](https://dynastynerds.com) | Rankings, rookie film work, and a dynasty-specific podcast |

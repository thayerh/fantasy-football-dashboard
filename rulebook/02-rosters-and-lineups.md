# 02 — Rosters & Lineups

## 2.1 Starting Lineup

Ten starters each week.

| Slot | Eligible positions |
|---|---|
| QB | QB |
| RB | RB |
| RB | RB |
| WR | WR |
| WR | WR |
| WR | WR |
| TE | TE |
| FLEX | RB / WR / TE |
| FLEX | RB / WR / TE |
| **SUPERFLEX** | **QB / RB / WR / TE** |

**No kickers. No team defenses.** Neither is rostered in this league at all.

The SUPERFLEX slot is what defines the format. You are not required to start two quarterbacks, but you almost always will — and that is the point. It makes QBs scarce, valuable, and worth drafting and trading for, which is the main thing 1QB dynasty leagues get wrong.

**This is a deep lineup, on purpose.** Ten starters across twelve teams means 120 starting slots every week, including 36 mandatory wide receiver slots before either flex is filled. Two consequences worth knowing before the startup draft:

- **Wide receiver is the scarcest position after quarterback.** A team's third WR is, leaguewide, roughly the 36th-best receiver — a range that in shallower leagues is waiver fodder. Draft accordingly.
- **Your bench is sized to cover this lineup, not to speculate with** (see §2.2). Thirteen spots behind ten starters is a little over one backup apiece — enough to survive a stacked bye week, not enough to hoard. The waiver wire still matters more here than it would in a league starting eight.

**Illegal lineups.** A lineup with an empty starting slot, or with a player on bye, scores zero for that slot. Failing to set a legal lineup is an inactivity and integrity issue, not just a bad week — see [Governance §9.3](09-governance-and-conduct.md#93-tanking-and-lineup-integrity).

## 2.2 Roster Limits

| Roster area | Slots |
|---|---|
| Starters | 10 |
| Bench | 13 |
| **Active roster** | **23** |
| Taxi squad | 3 |
| Injured Reserve | 3 |
| **Maximum players controlled** | **29** |

Thirteen bench spots behind ten starting slots leaves a workable margin — a little over one backup per starter. It keeps 276 players rostered leaguewide rather than 300, so real talent still circulates through free agency and a rebuild remains executable, while giving you enough depth to survive a stacked bye week without gutting your lineup.

**Compliance.** Rosters must be legal (at or under every limit) at these moments:

- **Kickoff of NFL Week 1** — the hard annual cutdown.
- **Every waiver processing run** — Sleeper will not process a claim that would put you over the limit unless you attach a drop.

Rosters may exceed the active limit during the offseason *only* between the start of the rookie draft and Week 1 kickoff, and only because of rookie draft selections. While over the limit, Sleeper locks your starting lineup and blocks free agent claims — harmless in May, fatal in September.

Any team over the limit at Week 1 kickoff has players dropped by the commissioner, lowest-rostered-percentage first, after one warning.

## 2.3 Taxi Squad

Three taxi slots. Players on the taxi squad do not count against the 23-man active roster and **cannot be started**. This is your developmental stash.

**Eligibility: rookies only.** First-year players, in the season they are drafted — Sleeper setting: max experience 1 year, non-rookies disabled. The taxi squad does exactly one job in this league, which is warehousing the current rookie class.

**Rules of the taxi squad.**

1. **Waiver and free agent claims cannot go directly to taxi.** Sleeper requires a claimed player to land on your active roster first, then be moved to taxi. You need an open active spot to make the claim.
2. **Rookie draft picks are the exception.** You may draft over the roster limit and move your rookies to taxi afterward — no pre-draft cutdown required. See [Rookie Draft §6.1](06-rookie-draft.md#61-format).
3. **The taxi deadline is kickoff of NFL Week 1** — except in the inaugural season, when it is **kickoff of NFL Week 2**. After the deadline passes, no player may be moved *onto* taxi for the rest of the season.
4. **Promotion is one-way and permanent.** You may promote a taxi player to your active roster at any time, but once promoted after the deadline they can never return to taxi that season.
5. **Promoting requires an open active roster spot.** If you are at 23, you must drop or trade someone first.
6. **Taxi players are yours.** Sleeper does not support taxi raiding or poaching, and this league does not either. Taxi players may be traded normally.
7. **Taxi players may be dropped**, and go through waivers like anyone else.
8. **Eligibility resets at the end of each season.** A player whose experience has aged past the limit must be promoted to the active roster before Week 1 or dropped.

> **The inaugural season gets one extra week.** In year one only, the taxi deadline is kickoff of **Week 2** rather than Week 1. The startup draft is 20 rounds run in a single 2.5-hour sitting, and the rookies you take in it arrive alongside 20 other players with no offseason to sort them — one extra week to work out which three belong on taxi is a reasonable allowance for a league that has existed for a few weeks. From the second season onward the deadline is Week 1 kickoff, permanently.
>
> **The roster compliance deadline does not move.** Even in year one you must be at or under every limit at **Week 1 kickoff** (§2.2). The extra week buys you time to decide *which* players go to taxi, not permission to carry an oversized roster into the season. A player whose NFL game has already kicked off in Week 1 is locked and cannot be moved that week.

> **On mid-season promotion.** Rules 3 and 4 are the ones that surprise people new to dynasty. The taxi deadline restricts one direction only — you may promote a taxi player into your lineup at any point in the season, but you can never send him back. Sleeper enforces this and it is not configurable: *"Players can always be moved out of taxi slots onto the bench or starting positions in the season."* See [Sleeper — How do taxi squads work?](https://support.sleeper.com/en/articles/3640482-how-do-taxi-squads-work).
>
> This is deliberate, not a loophole. If taxi players were frozen for the year, taxi slots would be strictly worse bench spots and stashing a rookie who breaks out in Week 6 would punish you for being right. The cost of the taxi squad is that the player scores nothing while he sits, that promoting him costs an active roster spot, and that the move is irreversible — not that he is unusable.

## 2.4 Injured Reserve

Three IR slots. IR players do not count against the active roster limit.

**Eligibility is enforced by the platform, not by opinion.** A player may occupy an IR slot only while Sleeper lists them with an official NFL designation of **IR, PUP, NFI, COVID, or Suspended**. "Doubtful," "Out," and "Questionable" do not qualify.

- When a player's designation clears, you have **48 hours** to move them to your active roster or drop them. Sleeper flags this automatically.
- A roster that is illegal because of a cleared IR player cannot process waiver claims.
- IR players may be traded.

## 2.5 Player Eligibility

- A player's position is whatever **Sleeper** lists. Position changes mid-season follow the platform; the commissioner does not adjudicate them.
- Only players on an NFL roster, practice squad, or the current year's NFL draft class may be rostered. College players who have not declared are **not** eligible — this is not a devy league.
- Retired players may be held on IR only if the NFL/Sleeper designation supports it; otherwise they must be dropped.

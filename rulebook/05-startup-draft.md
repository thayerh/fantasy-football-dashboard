# 05 — Startup Draft

The inaugural draft. This happens once, and it is the single most consequential event in the league's history — every asset every team will ever own traces back to it.

## 5.1 Format

**Snake (serpentine).** Order reverses each round: the team picking 1st in round one picks 12th in round two, 1st again in round three, and so on.

**Rounds: 20.** The startup does not quite fill your roster. Each team leaves the draft with 20 players and fills its remaining **3 roster spots** through FAAB before Week 1 — see [§5.5](#55-after-the-draft).

This is a deliberate concession to the calendar. A startup that fills all 23 roster spots is 276 picks, and 276 picks cannot be drafted well in 2.5 hours — that is under 33 seconds per pick with no allowance for early rounds taking longer. Round count is the only lever that buys real thinking time where it matters, and 20 is as far as it stretches: at 240 picks the tiered clock below lands at 2h 13m, and every round past that eats into the technical-pause budget. The rounds you lose are 21 through 23, which in any startup are close to indistinguishable from waiver-wire claims anyway.

**Player pool:** all NFL offensive players (QB/RB/WR/TE) **plus the current year's incoming rookie class**.

**The incoming rookie class is in the startup pool.** Every asset sits on one board and everyone competes for the same players. There is **no separate rookie draft in year one** — the first rookie draft happens the following spring.

## 5.2 Draft Order

**The draft order is set by the commissioner.** It is determined by a secret challenge run by the commissioner, and announced before the draft begins.

The order is final once announced. The commissioner does not have to explain the mechanism, but the order must be posted in the league chat before the first pick, and it does not change afterward for any reason.

**Draft slots may not be traded.** The slot you are assigned is the slot you draft from. It cannot be sold, swapped, or packaged into a trade at any point.

The order is the outcome of the secret challenge, and a prize that can be bought is not a prize. This also removes the one pre-draft negotiation that no owner has any information with which to price — before a single pick has been made, nobody in a brand-new league knows what the 1.01 slot is actually worth.

**Straight snake, no third-round reversal.** Rounds run 1→12, 12→1, 1→12, 12→1, and so on for all 20 rounds. The order the secret challenge produces is a real prize, and softening it would defeat the purpose of playing for it.

## 5.3 Timing

**Format: live draft, one sitting, 2 hours 30 minutes maximum.**

The clock is **tiered** across three blocks — early picks get real time, late picks move fast. Rounds 1–3 are where the league is actually built; rounds 11–20 are lottery tickets and should be drafted like it.

| Rounds | Picks | Clock per pick | Worst-case time |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1–3** | 36 | **90 sec** | 54m |
| **4–10** | 84 | **35 sec** | 49m |
| **11–20** | 120 | **15 sec** | 30m |
| **Total** | **240** | | **2h 13m** |

**No scheduled break.** The draft runs start to finish in one sitting. Worst case is **2h 13m**, 17 minutes under the 2.5-hour ceiling — which is exactly the room needed to absorb the full technical-pause budget below and still finish on time. Handle food, drinks, and anything else before the first pick.

The table assumes *every owner burns their entire clock on every pick*, which never happens. A live draft with prepared owners typically consumes 50–65% of the clock, so the realistic finish is **90 minutes to 1 hour 45**.

**Autopick on expiry: yes** — best player available by Sleeper ranking.

### How the tiered clock is run

Sleeper has a single pick-timer setting, but the commissioner can change it while the draft is live. Three tiers means **two changes**, both announced in the draft chat one round in advance:

| Before round | Set timer to |
|---|---|
| **4** | 35 sec |
| **11** | 15 sec |

Changing the timer does not affect a pick already on the clock.

### What owners must do beforehand

A 2.5-hour draft only works if people arrive prepared. This is not optional:

1. **Queue at least 45 players** in the Sleeper app before the draft starts. Twenty rounds means 240 players come off the board, so a shallow queue is empty by round 14. In rounds 11–20 you have 15 seconds; that is enough time to confirm a queued pick and nothing else.
2. **Draft from a computer or tablet**, not a phone, if you have the option.
3. **Be in the room five minutes early.** The draft starts on time.
4. **Know your Superflex plan before pick one.** Round 1 is 90 seconds — enough to react to a surprise, not enough to form a strategy.

### Pauses

The commissioner may pause the draft for a **technical failure only** — a platform outage or an owner disconnecting. Pauses are capped at **5 minutes each and 15 minutes total**, and that budget is inside the 2.5-hour ceiling.

The draft is **not** paused for deliberation, and it is not paused for trades — [there are no trades](#54-no-trading-until-the-startup-concludes).

### If an owner cannot attend

Autopick handles it. An absent owner's queue drafts for them, and a queue built in advance will produce a perfectly reasonable roster. An owner who knows they will be absent should tell the commissioner and queue 65+ players rather than 45.

## 5.4 No Trading Until the Startup Concludes

**No trades of any kind are permitted until the startup draft is over.** Not before it, not during it. This covers players, draft slots, individual picks within the startup, and FAAB.

Trading opens the moment the final pick of the startup is made, and from then on runs under the normal [trade rules](08-trades.md).

Two reasons. First, a 2.5-hour live draft has no room in it to negotiate and process a trade — a single mid-draft trade would eat the entire slack budget. Second, nobody in a brand-new league can price anything yet: before a single pick is made, no owner has any basis for valuing the 1.01 slot, a 2028 second-rounder, or a player they have never rostered. The trades that would get made in that window are the ones people regret for years.

## 5.5 After the Draft

- **Trading opens immediately**, under the normal [trade rules](08-trades.md).
- **FAAB budgets are issued** — $100 per team. See [Waivers §7.1](07-waivers-and-free-agency.md#71-faab-budget).
- **Teams are 3 players short of a full roster** and fill the gap through FAAB. All undrafted players enter the free agent pool and are claimed through the normal daily waiver process.
- Budget accordingly: the preseason roster-filling period is the first real call on your $100, and an owner who spends aggressively to finish their roster in August has little left for the Week 3 waiver break-out.
- Rosters must be legal at **Week 1 kickoff**, not at the end of the draft.
- The taxi squad opens immediately. Because this is the inaugural season, rookies may be moved to taxi any time up to **kickoff of Week 2** — one extra week, granted only in year one ([Rosters §2.3](02-rosters-and-lineups.md#23-taxi-squad)). Your roster must still be legal at **Week 1 kickoff**.

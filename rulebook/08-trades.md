# 08 — Trades

Trading is the best part of a dynasty league. The rules here are deliberately permissive: the default answer to "can I trade this?" is yes.

## 8.1 What Can Be Traded

- Players on the active roster, taxi squad, or IR
- Rookie draft picks, within the [pick-trading window](#83-trading-draft-picks)

## 8.2 What Cannot Be Traded

- **Future considerations**, "I'll get you back next year," handshake side deals, or anything not fully executable on the platform at the moment the trade is accepted. If it can't be entered into Sleeper, it isn't a trade.
- **Real money**, in any direction, for any reason.
- **FAAB dollars.** See §8.4.
- **Loans of any kind**, including FAAB lent against a future budget.
- **Anything belonging to a third team.** You can only trade what you currently own.
- **Anything at all, until the startup draft is over.** Trading is closed until the final pick of the inaugural draft is made — no players, no draft slots, no picks within the startup, no FAAB. The startup draft order is set by the commissioner and is not for sale. See [Startup Draft §5.4](05-startup-draft.md#54-no-trading-until-the-startup-concludes).

Every trade must be executed through Sleeper. An agreement reached in a group chat is not binding until it is accepted in the app.

## 8.3 Trading Draft Picks

**Window: the current league year plus the next two.** As of any moment, you may trade picks in up to three rookie drafts.

> Example: after the 2027 rookie draft concludes, teams may trade 2028, 2029, and 2030 picks. The 2030 picks become tradeable the moment the 2027 draft ends.

Three years is the standard. It is long enough for a real rebuild to be financed and short enough that a departing owner cannot mortgage a decade and vanish.

**Dues requirement.** *An owner who has not paid the current year's dues may not trade a future draft pick in either direction.* No exceptions. This is the mechanism that keeps future picks worth something.

**Traded picks are tracked** by the commissioner and reflected in Sleeper's draft pick ledger. If the platform and the commissioner's records disagree, the accepted trade in Sleeper's transaction log governs.

## 8.4 FAAB Is Not Tradeable

**FAAB dollars may not be traded, sold, gifted, or included in a trade in any form.** Your $100 is yours alone, for the whole league year.

FAAB trading is common in redraft leagues and it works badly in dynasty. A rebuilding team has no use for waiver money and every incentive to convert it into future picks, which quietly turns the budget into a second currency that contenders can buy — and the teams doing the selling are the same teams already under suspicion of tanking. Keeping FAAB non-transferable means every team's waiver budget reflects that team's own decisions, and a $47 winning bid means the same thing no matter who made it.

## 8.5 Trade Deadline

**Trading closes at kickoff of the first game of Week 12** and reopens **immediately after the championship game concludes**.

Week 12 is the standard deadline and it lands in the right place: teams know by then whether they are contending, and the three remaining regular-season weeks are played out with the rosters everyone finished the deadline holding. Nobody sells a starter to a rival in Week 14 and reshapes a playoff race they are no longer in.

**Offseason trading is unrestricted** from the end of the championship game through Week 12 of the following season, subject to the dues rule in §8.3.

**Startup year:** trading opens for the first time when the final pick of the [startup draft](05-startup-draft.md#54-no-trading-until-the-startup-concludes) is made, and runs until the Week 12 deadline.

## 8.6 Collusion

**Trades process automatically. There is no league vote and no veto by owners.**

This is deliberate. Veto votes in fantasy leagues are almost always used by owners who dislike a trade's *value*, not its *integrity*, and a league where a good rebuild trade can be voted down is a league where nobody rebuilds.

**The commissioner may reverse a trade only for collusion**, defined as:

- A transfer of value intended to benefit one team at the expense of the league, without genuine competitive consideration
- A trade made to circumvent roster, FAAB, or dues rules
- A trade involving any consideration outside the league (money, favors, side deals)
- A trade by an owner who has announced or intends to abandon their team

**Not collusion:** a trade you think is bad. A lopsided-looking trade between a contender and a rebuilder. An owner overpaying for a player they love. A trade that helps your rival. Dynasty valuations differ enormously and that is the game.

**Process.** Any owner may raise a collusion concern to the commissioner within 48 hours of a trade processing. The commissioner investigates, hears from both parties, and rules. If the commissioner is a party to the trade, the co-commissioner rules. A reversal requires the commissioner to state the specific factual basis in the league chat.

**Penalties for confirmed collusion**, at the commissioner's discretion, escalating: trade reversal → forfeiture of the next rookie first-round pick → removal from the league by a 9-of-12 vote, with dues forfeited.

## 8.7 Trades and Vacant Teams

A team whose owner has been removed or has abandoned it is **frozen**: no trades in or out until a new owner is installed. Any trade accepted by an owner who is subsequently removed for abandonment within the following 14 days is subject to commissioner review.

A new owner inherits the roster and all pick obligations exactly as they are. Trades made by the previous owner are not undone.

## 8.8 The Annual Trade Requirement

**Every owner must complete at least one trade each season to be eligible for a payout.**

A team that finishes in the money without having made a trade that year does not collect. This is an engagement rule, not a competitive one — a dynasty league dies when half the owners stop transacting, and the season-long silence usually starts with one or two teams that set a lineup and never speak.

**What counts.** One completed trade with another team in this league, processed on Sleeper, involving at least one player or draft pick. Waiver claims, free agent adds, and taxi or IR moves are not trades. One trade is enough — there is no credit for more and no penalty for none beyond the payout forfeiture.

**The window.** From the opening of the league year on **February 1** through the [Week 12 trade deadline](#85-trade-deadline). A trade made after your own season has ended but before the deadline still counts. Year one is the exception: the window opens when the startup draft ends, because [trading is closed until then](05-startup-draft.md#54-no-trading-until-the-startup-concludes).

**December and January trades do not count.** Trading reopens the moment the championship ends ([§8.5](#85-trade-deadline)), but the league year does not turn over until February 1. A trade made in that gap counts toward neither season — not the one just finished, whose payouts are already settled, and not the one to come. If you want the requirement satisfied, trade on or after February 1. This is a real window of a few weeks, so it is worth knowing about rather than discovering in December.

**Sham trades do not count.** Swapping a 2029 fourth for a 2029 fourth to tick the box is not a trade, and neither is any exchange arranged for the sole purpose of satisfying this section. The commissioner may rule a trade non-qualifying for this purpose without reversing it — the trade stands, it just does not buy eligibility. A trade later reversed for collusion under [§8.6](#86-collusion) never counted in the first place.

**Forfeited shares pass down.** If an ineligible team finishes in a payout position, that share goes to the next eligible team in the same category: champion to runner-up, runner-up to third place, most regular-season points to the next-highest eligible scorer. Money never leaves the pool and never returns to the commissioner.

**Warning.** The commissioner posts a list of teams that have not yet traded in the league chat after Week 8 and again in the week before the deadline. Nobody should lose a payout to a rule they forgot, but the reminder is a courtesy — the requirement stands whether or not it is posted.

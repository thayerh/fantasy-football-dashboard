# 09 — Governance & Conduct

A redraft league that goes badly is over in four months. A dynasty league that goes badly poisons a decade. This section exists because the rules that keep a dynasty league alive are not the rules about football.

## 9.1 Amendments

**When.** Rule change proposals may be submitted between **February 1 and March 1** each year — after the season ends, before dues are due and before the rookie draft. There is no other amendment window.

**No mid-season rule changes.** None, for any reason, including obviously good ideas. The only exception is correcting a platform setting that does not match this document, which the commissioner does immediately and announces.

**Process.**
1. Any owner submits a written proposal to the league chat before March 1.
2. Discussion period of at least 1 day.
3. The commissioner calls a vote with a stated deadline of at least 24 hours.
4. Passed amendments take effect for the coming season and are written into this rulebook, with the change noted in [the change log](#97-change-log).

**A non-vote counts as a vote against.** An owner who does not respond before the stated deadline is recorded as opposed. This applies to **every league vote** — amendments, expulsions, commissioner removal, and approving a new owner alike. Silence defeats proposals, so an owner who wants the league to be able to change anything has to actually vote. See [§9.2](#92-voting).

## 9.2 Voting

**Every league vote requires 9 of 12 owners in favor, with one exception.** A scoring tweak, a roster change, expelling an owner, and removing the commissioner all clear the same bar. Approving a new owner for a vacant team does not.

| Decision | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Any rule amendment | **9 of 12** |
| Scoring change | **9 of 12** |
| Roster size, starting lineup, or Superflex status | **9 of 12** |
| League size change | **9 of 12** |
| Rookie draft order method | **9 of 12** |
| Expelling an owner | **9 of 12** |
| Removing the commissioner | **9 of 12** |
| **Approving a new owner for a vacant team** | **Simple majority** of the remaining active owners — 6 of 11 with one team vacant ([§9.5](#95-vacant-teams-orphans)) |

**Why orphan approval is the exception.** Every other vote on this list changes the rules, and stability is worth more than speed. Filling a vacant team is the opposite: it is time-sensitive, the alternative to approving a candidate is an unmanaged roster sitting in the league, and a 9-of-12 bar would let three quiet owners leave a team empty through the offseason. A simple majority is enough to reject someone the league genuinely does not want.

The bar is set high deliberately. In a dynasty league the rules *are* asset values — a change to the passing touchdown value or the starting lineup reprices every roster in the league and rewards whoever saw it coming. Requiring near-consensus means an owner can trade for a 2029 pick without worrying that seven people will quietly change what that pick is worth.

The cost is real and worth naming: a genuinely broken rule can survive because four owners could not be bothered to vote. Two mitigations follow from that.

**A non-vote counts as a vote against.** This is stated in [§9.1](#91-amendments) and applies to every vote in the table above, including the simple-majority one. It matters much more under a 9-of-12 threshold than it would under a majority — silence defeats proposals. Owners who want the league to be able to fix itself have to actually show up.

**The commissioner votes as an owner** and has no tiebreaking or veto power on amendments.

## 9.3 Tanking and Lineup Integrity

**Every owner must field a complete, legal, healthy starting lineup every week of the season, including weeks in which they are eliminated, on a bye in the toilet bowl, or playing a meaningless game.**

Specifically prohibited:

- Leaving a starting slot empty
- Starting a player on a bye week, or listed **Out** or on **IR**, when a healthy alternative is on your roster
- Dropping productive players to weaken your own team
- Acquiring or starting players for the purpose of helping or harming another team's playoff position
- Refusing to engage in trades or waivers for the purpose of finishing lower
- Treating a **placement game** as meaningless. The 5th and 3rd place games decide picks 1.07–1.10 ([Schedule §4.2](04-schedule-and-playoffs.md#42-playoffs)); a team eliminated from the championship is still playing for something every remaining week.

**Not prohibited:** trading veterans for picks, starting young players over established ones to evaluate them, or building for next year. **Rebuilding is legitimate and expected. Sabotage is not.** The distinction is whether you are trying to *win the games you are playing* with the roster you have chosen to assemble.

**Enforcement.**

| Offense | Consequence |
|---|---|
| First illegal or incomplete lineup | Written warning in the league chat |
| Second | Forfeiture of the team's highest remaining rookie pick after round 1 |
| Third | Forfeiture of the team's next first-round rookie pick |
| Pattern of deliberate tanking | Expulsion vote (9 of 12), dues forfeited |

The commissioner enforces §9.3 on their own initiative, not only on complaint.

## 9.4 Inactivity

An owner is **inactive** if any of the following occur:

- Two consecutive weeks with an incomplete starting lineup
- Seven days without responding to a direct message from the commissioner during the season, or fourteen days during the offseason
- Failing to respond to a leaguewide vote or a scheduling poll twice in the same league year

**Process.** Warning in the league chat and by direct message → seven days to respond → if unresolved, the commissioner declares the team **vacant** and the roster is frozen (§8.7).

**Unpaid dues run on their own clock, not this one.** An owner who misses the March 1 dues deadline gets one written warning and **seven days from March 1** — March 8, not a second grace period stacked on top of the first. If the dues are not paid by then the team is vacant. See [Structure §1.4](01-league-structure.md#14-dues).

An owner who knows they will be unavailable (travel, illness, life) should tell the commissioner in advance and name a temporary lineup-setter. Announced absences are not inactivity.

**Trading is participation.** Separately from this section, every owner must complete at least one trade per season to be eligible for a payout — see [Trades §8.8](08-trades.md#88-the-annual-trade-requirement). Going a season without a single trade is not by itself inactivity, but it is usually the first visible sign of it.

## 9.5 Vacant Teams (Orphans)

Vacant teams are the leading cause of death for dynasty leagues. This league treats filling them as the commissioner's highest-priority duty.

**Filling a vacancy, in order of preference:**

1. **The departing owner finds their own replacement.** Owners leaving voluntarily are expected to do this, and to give the commissioner at least 30 days' notice. This is by far the best outcome.
2. **A friend of an existing owner**, nominated in the league chat. Every owner should be asked before the search goes any wider — a candidate somebody already knows is worth more than a stranger with a better résumé.
3. **A public search**, run by the commissioner. Post the opening where dynasty players actually look, screen candidates yourself, and share the roster and this constitution with anyone who asks. Do not hand a team to the first person who replies.

If a vacancy cannot be filled by the start of the rookie draft, the commissioner runs the team under **interim management** below until it can be. A well-managed empty seat is survivable for a season; a bad owner is not.

**Terms for an incoming owner.**

- The new owner takes the roster and all draft-pick obligations **exactly as they are**. No roster reset, no restocking, no compensatory picks by default. This is the standard, and it is what keeps existing owners' assets honest — a rebuilt orphan devalues every pick anyone traded for.
- The new owner pays the full annual dues for the current year. If the departing owner already paid, the incoming owner reimburses them.
- New owners must be approved by a **simple majority of the remaining active owners** — 6 of 11 while one team is vacant. This is the one league vote that does not require 9 of 12; see [§9.2](#92-voting). A non-vote counts against, so owners should answer the approval poll promptly.

**Distressed teams.** If a vacant team is genuinely uncompetitive — a bottom-two roster with few future picks — it may be unfillable on standard terms. In that case the commissioner may declare the team distressed and award the incoming owner **one additional second-round rookie pick** in the next rookie draft, inserted at the end of round 2 rather than taken from another team.

This is applied only by commissioner declaration, must be announced **before the team is advertised**, and is used sparingly. A compensatory pick costs the league no cash and does not touch anyone's existing assets; the trade-off is that it lengthens round 2 and can feel unfair to the team that finished just above the orphan in the standings. If the commissioner is declaring teams distressed more than about once every few years, the problem is league health, not orphan terms.

**Interim management.** While a team is vacant, the commissioner sets its lineup each week using Sleeper's optimal-lineup projection. The team makes no trades and no waiver claims.

## 9.6 Conduct

- Do not ghost. If you want out, say so and help find a replacement. Leaving well costs you nothing and leaving badly is remembered for years.
- Answer trade offers, even to decline. A one-word "no thanks" within a day or two is the entire social contract of a dynasty league.
- Disputes go to the commissioner privately first, not to the league chat.

Repeated conduct violations are grounds for an expulsion vote (9 of 12). An expelled owner forfeits their dues and their team is handled as a vacancy.
